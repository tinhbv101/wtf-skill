# Handling pushback

Use this only after a roast, when the user argues back. Answer shorter than the first roast (100-250 words), same language and tone. Figures come from `figures.md` - don't add new ones here.

The rule: **hold the line on facts, concede on scope.** If they bring a new fact or a smaller scope, update the WTF-meter and switch to helping. If they only repeat the hype, answer the argument once and move back to the way out.

## "Instagram had 13 employees when it sold for $1B"

- True, and it proves the opposite: that was roughly 18 months after launch, by strong engineers, with tens of millions of users already earned - and it then ran on Facebook's infrastructure.
- Small team, yes. One hour, no. Ask what their 18-month plan is.

## "WhatsApp had ~55 people serving hundreds of millions"

- About 5 years of work, founders who were long-time Yahoo engineers, and a specialist messaging stack (Erlang). The small team was the result of deep expertise, not a replacement for it.

## "Solo founders make millions with AI apps" / indie hacker stories

- The famous solo builders ship **small, niche products**, many of which fail before one works (Pieter Levels' "12 startups in 12 months" is the pattern, not the exception). They are not cloning Uber.
- If the user wants that path, great: that's a realistic plan - pick one niche problem, ship small, repeat.

## "Lovable / Bolt / v0 / Cursor built it in 5 minutes"

- It built the visible tip: screens and CRUD. Walk them back through the waterline list and tick which items the tool did. Usually zero.
- These tools are genuinely good at prototypes - use them for exactly that.

## "AI will be 10x better in 6 months"

- Then it will be 10x better for every competitor too, and for everyone who wants to clone *them*. When code gets cheap, what's scarce is users, trust, distribution and operations - exactly the parts no model ships for you.

## "I just need a demo for investors"

- Now we're talking - a demo is realistic. **Re-score the WTF-meter down.**
- One caveat: early-stage investors mostly fund teams and traction. A demo plus 20 users who actually use it beats a perfect-looking demo.

## "I'm not cloning it, just a simpler version"

- Good. Make them say which *one* loop and for *which* group of people. If they can answer that in one sentence, the roast is over - help them build it.

## "You're just gatekeeping / old-school engineers are scared"

- Not saying don't build - saying build the right-sized thing. The roast is about the deadline and the scope, not about whether they're allowed to code.

## "Company X was built by a tiny team with AI"

- Ask for specifics: how many years, what niche, what prior experience? Almost always: years of work, a narrow product, founders who already knew the domain. If you can't verify the story, don't argue numbers - argue the pattern.

## "Code is free now, so I win by moving first"

- If code is free, it's free for everyone: a copycat can clone you in the same hour. First-mover advantage comes from users and distribution, not from being first to generate code.
